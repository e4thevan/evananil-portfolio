A / B: the existing tools and the proposal

This is a desk-based feature and interaction assessment using Metrobus’s published guidance and inspection of Townie. It is not a randomized A/B test, a hands-on audit of every native m-Mobile screen, or evidence that an undocumented feature is absent.

Global patterns, adapted to St. John’s

  • Transit, North America: contextual guidance during the ride and a clear next action. Townie uses a trip-specific timeline and opt-in reminders. It does not claim Transit's native background delivery or crowdsourced positioning. Transit GO.
  • TfL Go, London: bring arrivals, disruptions and accessibility into journey decisions. Townie surfaces service notices and honest access labels; verified accessible routing remains a data and validation requirement. TfL Go.
  • MyTransport, Singapore: make everyday arrival and journey information practical and easy to reach. Townie keeps fares and assistance close, but does not invent crowding or vehicle-accessibility indicators absent from its data. Singapore LTA.
  • Accessible interaction: use text and icons as well as color, visible keyboard focus, generous controls and reduced motion. These are design measures, not certification. W3C: use of color; target size.

Design judgment: the strongest solution is not the most feature-heavy app. It is the smallest reliable sequence that answers “which bus?”, “how certain is that time?” and “what should I do next?”

Local requirements that changed the design

Metrobus’s accessibility consultation identifies ETA reliability, detours and temporary stops, snow clearing and inclusive information as concerns. It also cautions against relying on mobile technology alone. Townie responds with data-confidence labels, journey alerts, winter guidance and a visible phone-support route. An app cannot clear a sidewalk or guarantee a boarding space. Metrobus consultation findings.

The included GTFS covers 7 September–1 November 2026. All 916 stop records have unknown accessibility in the bundled data; this is a dataset gap, not a statement that all Metrobus stops are inaccessible. The app labels it explicitly and needs a validated stop/vehicle source before offering accessible-trip guarantees. GTFS stop field definitions.

Newfoundland local-time handling and midnight behaviour are tested. Walking estimates remain straight-line approximations; hills, safe crossings, snow and sidewalks are not modeled.

What is implemented

Orange/teal interaction system; teal default buses and orange selection; numbered direction markers; fresh-GPS movement without extrapolation; location-aware stops; address autocomplete and saved places; direct-bus previews; stop timelines and connections; five-minute in-app trip reminders; service notices; Help and display settings; larger key text and reduced motion; a separate management/technical review screen.

Reliability changes include request timeouts, overlapping-poll protection, protection against responses for a previously selected stop, stale-position filtering, expired-schedule checks and service-suspension handling. Live responses are no longer cached offline as if newly current. The static app shell and schedule remain precached after installation. Analytics injection is disabled in this review edition.

What must not be represented as finished

  • Transfers, safe pedestrian routing, live sidewalk/snow conditions, verified accessible journeys and multilingual support.
  • Guaranteed background or locked-phone reminders, crowdsourcing, occupancy information or a dispatch console.
  • App-store publication, security/accessibility certification or measured rider benefits.
  • A contracted production feed, distributed rate-limit guarantee, incident-response process or automatic schedule-renewal pipeline.

A five-minute demonstration

  1. Open the rider view. Explain teal as available buses and orange as the selected bus; route numbers carry identity.
  2. Search a public landmark, inspect nearby stops and choose a departure. Show the direction and timing label.
  3. Preview a get-off stop, start a test trip without system notifications, then show Live tracking. Expand the panel and jump to the bus on the stop list.
  4. Open a stop’s connections and the service-update screen. Explain that a current alert or real service suspension takes precedence over the planned route.
  5. Open Help, then Prototype review. Show unknown stop accessibility and schedule validity as concrete integration questions.

Use a separate test browser profile for a clean demo. Do not show personal Home/Work addresses. Live vehicles and departures depend on service at the demonstration time; never insert simulated buses into the live screen without an explicit simulation label.

Validate before committing to rollout

Proposed pilot: recruit a small, diverse group including regular riders, newcomers, older riders and people with accessibility needs. Counterbalance A/B task order, use equivalent destinations and record service conditions. Ask participants to find a departure, identify its confidence, choose the correct direction, handle a detour and identify the get-off stop.

Measure unassisted task completion, time on task, wrong-direction selections, interpretation of stale information and perceived confidence. Validate ETA errors against observed arrivals separately from usability. Record device/network failures. Set acceptance thresholds jointly with Metrobus before the pilot; no results or uplift percentages are claimed in this proposal.

Suggested ownership: Metrobus validates operational data, official content and accessibility facts; the product team owns interaction quality and tests; a designated hosting/technical owner owns secrets, monitoring, caching, release rollback and schedule renewal.
