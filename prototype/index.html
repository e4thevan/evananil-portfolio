01 / THE OPPORTUNITY

Good information.
A more connected experience.

Metrobus already provides live tracking, departure information, alerts and rider support. Townie explores how these tools can feel like one continuous journey.

I started with the questions a rider actually asks: Which bus? How long? Am I going the right way? The goal is to make the next action clear, without filling a small screen with everything at once.

The prototype keeps official fares and customer support with Metrobus. It adds a focused interaction layer—not a competing payment system or dispatch tool.

02 / THE EXPERIENCE

Three moments.
One calmer journey.

01

Find your bus.

Search an address or landmark. Save Home and Work. See nearby stops and departures without needing to know a stop number.

Nearby-first
02

Understand the wait.

Route numbers identify buses. Teal is the default; orange means selected. Direction arrows and clear live-or-scheduled labels reduce guesswork.

Meaningful, not decorative
03

Know your next stop.

Follow a trip timeline, expand connections when needed and choose five-minute reminders. An expandable panel makes room for the route or the map.

Details on demand
A working prototype, with honest boundaries.

Live updates depend on Metrobus’s feed. Walking times are approximate. Transfer planning, verified accessible routing and guaranteed background reminders are not implemented. The review brief explains what a pilot would need.

03 / LOCAL BY DESIGN

Designed for St. John’s.
Not just for a nice screenshot.

01

Winter changes the journey.

Snow and sidewalks affect access. The prototype avoids presenting distance-based walking times as verified safe routes.

02

Unknown is not “accessible.”

Unconfirmed stop accessibility is labelled honestly. Larger key text, reduced motion and visible support options are part of the experience.

03

Trust matters more than animation.

Buses stop at their latest reported position. Old data does not become a confident live estimate just because it appears on a polished map.

04

People still need people.

Official guidance and Ride Guide remain easy to reach. The app is a supplement to rider support, not a replacement.

Informed by Metrobus’s accessibility consultation, alongside interaction patterns from Transit, TfL Go and Singapore’s MyTransport. References and the A/B assessment are in the design brief.

04 / LET’S BUILD ON IT

A useful starting point.
A practical next conversation.

I’m interested in working with Metrobus to test the idea with riders and turn the strongest parts into a maintainable, accessible experience.

For Metrobus

A tangible concept to evaluate, a focused rider-testing plan, and clear integration questions before committing to a wider rollout.

For riders

A simpler path from finding a departure to reaching a destination, with uncertainty made visible instead of hidden.

For the collaboration

A defined discovery or pilot engagement: agreed scope, responsibilities, success measures and a sustainable handover.
